# Two-Sentence Horror — Candidates

Judge criteria (in order): (1) genuinely frightening, exactly two sentences;
(2) sentence 2 turns/reveals something that makes sentence 1 worse on a re-read;
(3) horror implied, not explained; (4) precise, concrete prose, every word earns its place;
(5) original, not a familiar twist.

Banned cliches: "I was already dead", mirrors, dolls, "the call is coming from inside the house",
dead spouse simply returning, faceless men, smart devices gone evil.

## Candidate 1 — The warm cup
> Every evening, old Mr. Kwan set two cups of tea on his porch, one for himself and one for the wife he lost in the spring. Tonight, when he came out with the tray, the second cup was already warm.

- (1) Mild dread, quiet. (2) Strong reframe: someone/something has been drinking with him. (3) Fully implied. (4) Precise. (5) Weak — "dead wife returns" is a familiar trope.
- Verdict: good but familiar.

## Candidate 2 — The wrong ashes (CHOSEN)
> The vet called to say Biscuit's ashes were ready for pickup, and Daniel thanked her and hung up. Then he looked down at the dog asleep on his feet and realized he had never told the vet which of his two identical dogs was Biscuit.

- (1) Genuinely unsettling: a mundane phone call becomes dread. (2) Devastating reframe: sentence 1's ordinary errand may have cremated the wrong dog — or the warm weight on his feet is not his dog at all. (3) Nothing explained; the reader fills in the worst. (4) Concrete: vet, ashes, two identical dogs, warm weight on his feet. (5) Original — no familiar horror twist; the horror is a paperwork mistake with a body count.
- Verdict: best. The twist is specific, domestic, and impossible to un-read.

## Candidate 3 — Tomorrow's pills
> Every morning, Elias counted the pills in his mother's blister pack before she woke, and every morning the count was right. This morning two were missing, and the empty slots were the ones for tomorrow.

- (1) Creepy, slow dread. (2) Good reframe: someone is taking doses ahead of schedule — mother overdosing, or someone else in the house. (3) Implied. (4) Precise. (5) Fairly original.
- Verdict: strong, but the threat is vaguer than Candidate 2 and "someone in the house" is a well-worn fear.

## Candidate 4 — Crumbs in the bed
> Every night, Grandma Rosa set out a plate for her late husband at dinner, and every morning the plate was exactly as she left it. This morning the plate was licked clean, and there were crumbs on her side of the bed.

- (1) Chilling final image. (2) Strong reframe: the ritual guest has started sleeping beside her. (3) Fully implied. (4) Precise and concrete. (5) Weak — "dead husband returns" again, though the crumbs detail is fresh.
- Verdict: second best; loses to Candidate 2 on originality.

## Candidate 5 — Daddy came home
> Mara found her son's drawing of their family taped to the fridge, four stick figures instead of three. Underneath, in his careful handwriting, it said: "Daddy came home last night."

- (1) Frightening, but (5) "dead parent came home" is one of the most common two-sentence horror twists; a horror editor has read it a hundred times.
- Verdict: rejected on originality.

## Candidate 6 — The almost-her voice
> Nadia's smart speaker had never once misheard her in three years. This morning it answered a question she had only thought, in a voice that was almost hers.

- (1) Unsettling. (2) Good reframe. (3) Implied. (4) Precise. (5) Weak — "device reads minds / mimics voice" is a common tech-horror angle.
- Verdict: rejected on originality.

3. Candidate 3 — Tomorrow's pills
4. Candidate 1 — The warm cup
